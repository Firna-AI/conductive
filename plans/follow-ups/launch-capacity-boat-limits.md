# Launch demand can pass Boat's limits

- **Severity:** High. **Effort:** Small. **Confidence:** Medium. **Harder later:** No.
- **Kind:** Needs decision.
- **Added:** 2026-11-09, decision 214 of `plans/sandbox-provider-groundwork.md`, branch `calummoore/vercel-sandbox-provider`.
- **Location:** None. The capacity is a setting of the app's Boat account.
- **Reason:** Only the user can check and change the Boat plan and its spend. No groundwork task and no pull request of the groundwork depends on the answer.

- **Source:** Design. **Category:** Performance.
- **Terms:**
  - **Start:** each create or resume of a sandbox, also the sandbox of a build or a refresh.
  - **Boat organization:** the account space at Boat that holds the app's sandboxes and saved snapshots, with the API key that controls them.
- **Background:**
  - Current requirements, from the repository on 9 November 2026:
    - Every sandbox and every saved snapshot of every org runs in the app's own Boat account. All orgs share the limits of its plan, as the "Boat" section of `docs/architecture.md` says.
    - The runtime and the docs require a Boat organization that holds only the app's work, as "The app's Boat key" in `docs/protocol/access-and-security.md` and "The app's Boat account" in `worker/sandbox/README.md` say.
    - A new account waits for app access. An operator approval, an invite code, a referral code, or an org invitation lets the person in, as `docs/protocol/waitlist.md` says. A user with app access has no limit on cloud computers, because the product is free with no limits for each user.
    - The app's own work also uses starts: the first build of each new org, the daily refresh of each org, a refresh after a push at most every 30 minutes, and the restart near day 14 that keeps a stopped disk.
  - Boat figures as recorded on 9 November 2026. Nobody checked them again. Boat's limits and pricing pages are <https://docs.boat.dev/limits> and <https://docs.boat.dev/pricing>.
    - Boat's $20 plan runs 100 sandboxes at once, and at most 36 starts in a minute and 1,200 in a day with paid extra starts.
    - The $2000 plan runs 2,000 at once, and at most 270 starts in a minute and 14,400 in a day.
    - Each extra seat on the $2000 plan adds a full set of limits. The plan price comes back as compute time at $0.036 an hour.
  - History, not a verified current fact: the plan records of 5 November 2026 named the $20 plan. On 8 November 2026, the user kept the shared "Firna" Boat organization for the first deployment. The actual organization, plan, limits, and expected peak of the deployment are not recorded in the repository.
- **Problem:** The current Boat plan, its limits, and the expected launch peak are not recorded. A launch can need more starts or more running sandboxes than the plan allows.
- **Impact if no action:** At the limit, new and resumed workspaces fail, just when most new users arrive.
- **Root question:** How do new and resumed workspaces keep starting during the launch peak?
- **Options:** These are the options as recorded on 9 November 2026. A check of the options on that day added D, E, F, and G.
  - **A.** Move the Boat account to the plan that fits the expected peak, such as $500 or $2000. Size it by the starts in a minute as well as by the day. Turn on invoiced extra starts. Ask Boat for a custom plan if needed. No code. Risk: Boat's own machines can still run out, and a Boat outage still stops all starts. Effort: Small.
  - **B.** Build Vercel overflow before the launch. It cannot be ready before 13 November 2026, when Conductor Cloud goes read-only, and a rushed provider adds risk at the launch. Effort: Large.
  - **C.** Admit new users only as fast as capacity allows, with a waitlist or invite codes. It changes the rule that the product is free with no limits for each user. Effort: Medium. Since then, the waitlist and the codes exist, as `docs/protocol/waitlist.md` says.
  - **D.** Cut the app's own starts during the launch. Skip the daily refresh of an org with no workspace start in 7 days, and allow at most one refresh after a push in 2 hours. It changes the rules of "The org image" in `docs/protocol/org-image.md`. Effort: Medium.
  - **E.** Before the launch, give the app its own Boat organization on the plan that A sizes. On 8 November 2026, a follow-up item asked for such an organization as its option B: a separate organization and a key with only the app's actions, set as `BOAT_ORG_ID` and `BOAT_API_KEY` before the first users arrive, while existing sandboxes and snapshots stay in the old organization until they are deleted. Cost: a second Boat plan. Each existing org needs a new build, and test workspaces lose their sandboxes. It changes the choice of 8 November 2026 to keep the shared organization. Effort: Small.
  - **F.** Log a Worker warning when Boat's replies show few starts left, and at each `limit_reached` reply. Then raise the plan in time. Risk: a fast spike can pass the limit before a person acts. Effort: Small.
  - **G.** During the launch, stop idle sandboxes sooner, for example after 10 minutes. This frees running slots, and Boat does not count a resume that takes back a machine that it still holds. It changes the 30-minute idle stop of "Stop and resume" in `docs/protocol/workspaces-and-sandboxes.md`. Effort: Small.
- **Left out:**
  - Several workspaces in one sandbox, because it breaks the rule of one sandbox for each workspace, and it cannot be ready for the launch.
  - E2B for overflow, because E2B Pro ran only 100 sandboxes at once on 9 November 2026.
- **Wider fix:** None here. The structural fix is a second provider, which `plans/follow-ups/choose-second-sandbox-provider.md` chooses after the launch.
- **Previous examples:** On 5 November 2026, a check of the Boat limits found the limits of the $20 plan. An early choice set limits for each user, and a later choice removed them. On 8 November 2026, the user kept the shared organization for the first deployment.
- **Related:**
  - `plans/follow-ups/separate-boat-organization.md`: option E of this item is its option B. That item asks for the organization for isolation, and this item asks for it with a plan that fits the launch.
  - `plans/follow-ups/capacity-failure-reason.md`: what users see when a start reaches the limit.
  - `plans/follow-ups/choose-second-sandbox-provider.md`: the structural capacity fix after the launch.
- **Recommendation:** E, as recorded on 9 November 2026. It is A in an organization of the app's own: the app gets the whole plan, the key reaches only the app's sandboxes, and the move costs least before real users arrive. The first recommendation was A, and the check of the options recommended A. The runtime and the docs now require the app's own organization, so E becomes A once the deployment uses it. Before any action, check the current Boat plans, limits, and prices again, and size the plan from a fresh peak estimate.
- **Action:** ask the user, because only the user can change the Boat plan and its spend, and E changes the choice of 8 November 2026 to keep the shared organization.
